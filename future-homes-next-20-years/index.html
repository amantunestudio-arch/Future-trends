What Will Homes Look Like in the Next 20 Years?

18 Nov 2026 · 3 min read

In this article
  1. Smart Homes Will Become Part of Everyday Life
  2. More Homes Will Generate Their Own Electricity
  3. Houses Will Be Designed to Save Energy
  4. Rooms Will Have More Than One Purpose
  5. Building a House Could Become Faster
  6. Water-Saving Features Will Become More Important
  7. Final Thoughts

Think about how much homes have changed over the last 20 years. Smartphones, smart TVs, home security cameras, and other technologies have become part of everyday life. Over the next two decades, our homes could change even more.

By 2046, houses may be smarter, more energy-efficient, and easier to adapt to different lifestyles. From appliances that understand daily routines to rooms that serve multiple purposes, future homes could make everyday living more comfortable and convenient.

Here are some changes we may see in the homes of the future.

1. Smart Homes Will Become Part of Everyday Life

Many people already use smart speakers, automatic lights, and connected security cameras. In the coming years, these features could become a normal part of home design.

Imagine arriving home after work and finding the lights switched on, the room at a comfortable temperature, and your favorite music playing. Smart systems may also learn daily routines and adjust appliances automatically.

For example, a smart thermostat could reduce heating or cooling when nobody is home and restore a comfortable temperature before residents return.

2. More Homes Will Generate Their Own Electricity

Electricity costs and interest in cleaner energy may encourage more homeowners to install solar panels and home battery systems.

Instead of depending entirely on the power grid, households could generate electricity during the day and store extra energy for nighttime use.

For example, a family with solar panels and a home battery could use stored electricity to run lights, fans, and other appliances after sunset. Electric vehicles may also become part of home energy systems by supplying stored power when compatible technology is available.

3. Houses Will Be Designed to Save Energy

Future homes may focus on saving energy right from the construction stage. Better insulation, larger energy-efficient windows, improved ventilation, and modern building materials could help keep indoor spaces comfortable throughout the year.

Smart sensors may also help homeowners understand where electricity is being wasted.

For someone living in a hot climate, for example, better insulation and properly designed windows could reduce the need to run air conditioning constantly. This could make homes more comfortable while lowering electricity bills.

4. Rooms Will Have More Than One Purpose

The way people use their homes has changed, especially with more people working and studying remotely. This is likely to influence future home designs.

Instead of having separate rooms for every activity, houses may include flexible spaces with movable partitions, foldable furniture, and built-in storage.

A small room could work as a home office during the day and become a guest bedroom when family members visit. This could be especially useful for people living in apartments with limited space.

5. Building a House Could Become Faster

New construction methods may change how houses are designed and built. Technologies such as 3D printing, prefabricated walls, and robotic construction equipment could reduce construction time and material waste.

Some companies already use large 3D printers to create parts of residential buildings. As these methods improve, they may offer new options for building affordable homes. However, costs, safety standards, and local building regulations will influence how widely they are adopted.

6. Water-Saving Features Will Become More Important

Water conservation could become a major part of future home design, particularly in areas that regularly experience water shortages.

Homes may use rainwater harvesting systems, smart leak detectors, water-efficient taps, and greywater recycling. Greywater systems can reuse treated water from showers and sinks for purposes such as watering gardens.

These features could help families reduce water waste without making major changes to their daily routines.

Final Thoughts

Homes in the next 20 years may not look completely different from the houses we live in today, but the way they function could change significantly. Smart appliances, solar energy, flexible rooms, and water-saving systems may become more common. The biggest change could be that future homes will do more to make everyday life comfortable while using fewer resources.

Quick answers

The questions people ask most.

Will future homes be fully automated?

Not completely. Smart systems may handle routine tasks, but people will still control how their homes operate.

Will smart homes become more affordable?

Many basic smart devices are already becoming more accessible. Advanced systems may still be expensive, depending on installation and features.

Will future homes use less electricity?

Many are expected to use less energy through improved insulation, efficient appliances, solar panels, and automated controls.

Will 3D-printed houses become common?

They could become more popular, but wider adoption will depend on construction quality, costs, safety standards, and building regulations.

A quick note

Smart-home products, solar panels and battery systems vary a lot in price, features and local rules. Check what is available and permitted in your area before you plan a purchase.
