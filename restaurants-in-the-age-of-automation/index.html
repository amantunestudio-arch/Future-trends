What Will Restaurants Look Like in the Age of Automation?

12 Nov 2026 · 3 min read

In this article
  1. Robots May Handle More Kitchen Tasks
  2. Customers Will Use More Self-Service Ordering Systems
  3. Artificial Intelligence Will Help Manage Restaurant Operations
  4. Automated Delivery Could Change Food Ordering
  5. Restaurant Employees Will Work Alongside Technology
  6. Dining Experiences May Become More Personalized
  7. Final Thoughts

Restaurants have always depended on people to prepare food, serve customers, and manage daily operations. But technology is gradually changing how these businesses work. Self-service kiosks, robotic kitchen equipment, digital ordering systems, and AI-powered tools are already appearing in restaurants around the world.

Over the next few years, automation could become a bigger part of the dining experience. Customers may place orders without speaking to staff, kitchens may use machines for repetitive tasks, and restaurant owners could manage inventory with smarter software. However, human workers will still play an important role in hospitality and food preparation.

Here are some ways restaurants may change as automation becomes more common.

1. Robots May Handle More Kitchen Tasks

Preparing food involves many repetitive activities, such as chopping ingredients, mixing, frying, and assembling meals. Automated machines and kitchen robots can already perform some of these tasks with consistent timing and portion sizes.

For example, a fast-food restaurant could use an automated fryer to prepare fries while kitchen staff focus on burgers, quality checks, and customer orders.

These machines may help restaurants serve food faster during busy hours. However, chefs will still be needed for recipe development, food presentation, and tasks that require experience and judgment.

2. Customers Will Use More Self-Service Ordering Systems

Self-service kiosks and digital menus are becoming familiar in many fast-food outlets. Instead of waiting for an employee to take an order, customers can browse the menu, customize their meals, and pay directly through a screen.

In the future, these systems may become more personalized. For example, a digital ordering system could remember a returning customer's previous order or suggest meals based on selected preferences.

This could reduce waiting times, although restaurants will still need staff to assist customers who prefer personal service.

3. Artificial Intelligence Will Help Manage Restaurant Operations

Restaurant owners deal with several daily challenges, including predicting customer demand, managing staff schedules, and controlling food costs. AI-powered software may make these tasks easier by analyzing sales records and customer patterns.

For example, if a restaurant regularly receives more orders on Friday evenings, its system could help managers prepare the right amount of ingredients and schedule additional staff.

Better predictions could reduce food waste and prevent shortages during busy periods.

4. Automated Delivery Could Change Food Ordering

Food delivery is another area where automation may have an impact. Restaurants already use digital platforms to receive orders and track deliveries. In some locations, delivery robots and autonomous vehicles are also being tested.

A restaurant in a busy urban area might eventually use small delivery robots for nearby orders, where local rules and infrastructure allow them.

Although these technologies are still developing, they could offer new delivery options and reduce dependence on traditional delivery methods.

5. Restaurant Employees Will Work Alongside Technology

Automation does not necessarily mean restaurants will operate without people. Employees will still handle customer complaints, maintain hygiene, check food quality, and provide the personal attention that makes dining enjoyable.

Their responsibilities may change as machines take over repetitive work. Staff could spend more time helping customers, managing automated equipment, and maintaining service standards.

6. Dining Experiences May Become More Personalized

Digital systems can help restaurants understand what customers order and which menu items are popular. In the future, restaurants may use this information to offer personalized recommendations, loyalty rewards, and customized promotions.

For example, a customer who regularly orders vegetarian meals might see suitable options first when opening a restaurant's ordering app.

Restaurants will need to handle customer information responsibly and provide clear privacy protections.

Final Thoughts

Restaurants in the age of automation will likely combine human skills with smarter technology. Robots may help prepare food, AI could improve daily planning, and digital systems may make ordering more convenient. However, good food, friendly service, and a welcoming atmosphere will remain important. The most successful restaurants may be those that use automation to improve the dining experience rather than remove the human touch.

Quick answers

The questions people ask most.

Will robots replace restaurant workers?

Not completely. Robots may handle repetitive tasks, but people will remain important for cooking, customer service, supervision, and problem-solving.

Will automated restaurants be cheaper to operate?

They may reduce some labor and food waste costs, but purchasing, installing, and maintaining automated equipment can be expensive.

Are restaurants already using automation?

Yes. Many restaurants use self-service kiosks, digital ordering, automated kitchen equipment, and inventory management software.

Will customers still prefer traditional restaurants?

Many customers will continue to value personal service, human interaction, and the atmosphere of traditional dining.

✎
A quick note

Automation is spreading unevenly across restaurants and countries. What you see in one city may not be available in another, so these changes will likely arrive at different speeds.
